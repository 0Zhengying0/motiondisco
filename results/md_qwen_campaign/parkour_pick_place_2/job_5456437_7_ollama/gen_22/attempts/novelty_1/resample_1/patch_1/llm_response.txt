<DESCRIPTION>
The previous implementations often failed due to improper handling of contact transitions, where contacts were changed without a free mode in between. This led to infeasible plans that the solver could not resolve. The new approach focuses on a structured sequence of movements that ensures all contacts are released before making new ones. This is achieved by carefully planning each step, including walking to the target, grasping the object, lifting it, moving to the destination, and placing it. The plan is designed to be as efficient as possible while adhering to the solver constraints.

Key insights:
1. Proper contact transition handling: Ensure that all contacts are released before making new ones.
2. Structured movement planning: Break down the task into clear steps, including walking, grasping, lifting, and placing.
3. Minimize modes: Reduce the number of modes to improve the feasibility of the plan and to meet the solver's mode cap.
4. Use of `walk` function: Utilize the `walk` function to efficiently move the robot while maintaining support.

This approach aims to create a more robust and feasible plan by addressing the common issues seen in previous implementations.
