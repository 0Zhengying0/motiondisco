Design one original indoor locomotion scene for a Unitree G1. Return exactly one JSON object conforming to the SceneSpec below.

Choose the objects, counts, supports, offsets, yaws, floor size, start and goal. Vary the layout concept, asset counts/types, repetition, support arrangement and direction of travel. You may use two copies of one asset with distinct instance IDs. Asymmetric layouts and placements of small boxes on a platform are encouraged when they suit the concept. Do not reuse an existing MotionDisco scene or change only its coordinates.

All distances are in metres and all yaw angles are in radians. The floor is centered at world (0,0,0); its half extents are half the length along x and half the width along y. The robot start and task target use world coordinates. Each object's relative_xy_yaw consists of x and y measured from its support face center in that face's local axes, then yaw relative to those axes. The object's bottom rests on the face; do not provide a height. Use floor.surface or another object's top. All supports must ultimately reach the floor. Fully contain the rotated box footprint within the declared support and within the floor projection. For each support axis, the absolute center offset plus the box footprint half extent along that axis must fit within the support half extent. For boxes sharing a support, leave enough separation between their footprints, using the wider support axis when it has more room. Keep the fixed platforms on fixed supports.

Include 0–2 platforms and 0–2 movable boxes, at least one object in total. Use lowercase unique scene and instance IDs. Reserve floor, robot, left_sole, right_sole, left_ee, and right_ee. The system supplies exactly one G1 with the FARO default standing configuration. It starts and ends on the floor. Put the start and target away from edges and objects. Prefer a clear walkable route and a starting yaw approximately facing the destination. Choose a goal 0.8–3 m away. Keep objects beside the route when their placement would otherwise obstruct it. Do not output a robot model, physics parameters, solver code or a declaration of validity. The program checks geometry, collisions and stability and will report specific errors for revision.
